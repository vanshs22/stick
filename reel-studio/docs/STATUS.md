## Style from a reference image or video (added last)
- `skills/reference-style/SKILL.md` teaches an AI to READ a style (character anatomy with ratios, materials, lighting from evidence, lens from depth, set inventory, colour roles, post, and for video the pace and sound), map it to what the engine can do, say honestly what it cannot, and prove the film matches.
- `tools/reference/style_brief.mjs` measures the look (light side, background blur, warm/cool, vignette, grain, face framing, skin and hair colour) and writes a sheet with a hint on every field; `--validate` refuses empty, vague, unlooked or colourless sheets. `style_map.mjs` picks the closest pack, writes `style-overrides.json` from the measured values and prints a per-element fidelity table (exact / close / approximate / CANNOT) with needs-build items and twist options. `style_check.mjs` is the by-eye protocol; `critics/style.mjs` measures family and palette shift and writes `review/style-compare-<ref>.jpg` (reference left, film right).
- **Worked example:** the stylised 3D podcaster image the user supplied, written up as `skills/reference-style/example-podcast-3d.style-brief.json`. The mapper picks `stylized-3d-presenter`, rates hair as CANNOT (volumetric clumps) and the face as approximate (no film-grade glossy eyes), and lists the objects the engine lacks (award plaque, neon sign, monitor, boom mic). The image file itself was not available in the workspace, so that brief has no measured block; the measuring tools were tested on frames of our own renders.
- **Before/after proof:** applying the brief's recipe to one shot (new character, tighter portrait framing, shallow depth of field, vignette, cooler light) visibly changed the render.
- Bugs found on the way: OpenCV 5 ships without the Haar face detector (requirements now pin `<5`, and the tool degrades honestly); `preview_shots.mjs` drew STALE stills after a storyboard edit (it now rebuilds the timeline first); the installer's module probe broke on a version-pinned requirement.
- Limits: faces and hair are simpler than a film-studio render (the skill tells the AI to design the twist around that: hood, beanie or short cut); a still-image reference cannot give pacing or sound; "character swap" and "set swap" are confirmed by eye, only the palette shift is measured.

## Honest limits
- **4K is an upscale.** Lanczos (ffmpeg) is instant and sharp-edged but adds no detail. Vector text and cards look best from a native render (`--scale 2`, about 4x the time). 3D shots are rendered at 1080x1920 and upscaled.
- **Voice emotion is shaped prosody, not acting.** Kokoro has no emotion control: speed, pitch, level, pauses, tremor and edge change per emotion (measured by `selftest_voice.mjs`). Punctuation in `tts_text` helps. A human still decides whether it sounds right.
- **Listening:** `tools/audio/ears.py` (free, built in) cuts clips and a checklist and flags masked sounds and level jumps; `tools/audio/earshot.mjs` wraps the open-source earshot analyser (CPU, about 1.1 GB models) for sync, loudness arc and professional-film comparison. Both measure; neither judges taste. The flags they raised on these films are minor (a level drop where the voice ends, a quiet pop).
- **Presenter-style captions** are small and sit at the top (the pack's `dialogue` preset keeps the host's face clear). That is a pack decision, not a bug.
- **3D characters are procedural rigs**, not modelled assets: presenter hair is a helmet in extreme close-up, voxel eyes are large.
- **Tamper evidence, not security:** approvals are signed in a chain, so hand-editing `state.json` is detected. An agent that reads the source could recompute it. The receipt records who approved and with which quote.
- **Speed:** 2D films build in about 1 minute; a 3D shot costs about 3 minutes without a GPU. Rendering uses all cores but two. Shots are cached by content hash.

## Windows (first real run of install.ps1)
It needed six fixes, all of which Linux could never show: dynamic `import()` of `C:\...` paths needs a `file://` URL (cinematography check, frames critic, build_video sync step, two self-tests); `npm`/`npx` are `.cmd` files and need a shell; directory links need junctions (a symlink needs admin); the bundled ffprobe stand-in was a `.cmd` the doctor could not run, so the installer now installs a real ffmpeg + ffprobe (`static-ffmpeg`); the newest Python on the machine has no onnxruntime or av wheel, so the installer prefers Python 3.10 to 3.13 and prints the version and pip's reason on failure; a hard-coded `.venv/bin/python` in one self-test. Windows paths with spaces are not tested; use a project folder without spaces.
